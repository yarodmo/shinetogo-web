Is window tint legal in Florida?

In Florida, tint on a passenger car must let at least 28% of visible light through the front side windows and at least 15% through the rear side and back windows. Multipurpose vehicles may go down to 6% behind the driver. The limit is measured on the finished window, glass plus film.

Florida limits how dark window tint can be (F.S. 316.2951–316.2957). The limit is measured on your car’s finished window, glass plus film. On passenger cars, front side windows must let in at least 28% of visible light, and rear side windows and the back window at least 15%. Vehicles the law classifies as multipurpose passenger vehicles (built on a truck chassis or with off-road features) may go down to 6% behind the driver. Reflectivity is capped too: 25% on front side windows and 35% behind the driver, so mirror-like film is not allowed. We confirm how your vehicle is classified before we recommend a film.

The windshield may only have a transparent strip along the top, above the AS-1 line marked on the glass. The installer must place a label on the inside of the driver-side door jamb with the film’s trade name and the installer’s business name. Tint that is too dark can mean a traffic ticket for the driver and having to remove the film, and it reduces visibility at night.

A vehicle with a Florida DHSMV sunscreening medical exemption certificate (F.S. 316.29545) may be darker. The certificate belongs to one vehicle and cannot be transferred. We install to the certificate you show us. We don’t give medical advice and we don’t file exemptions.

This is a summary, not legal advice. Rules can change, so confirm current requirements with the state or your installer before you order.

Questions, answered

Is window tint legal in Florida?

Yes, within limits. Florida sets a minimum amount of light that must pass through each window, measured on the finished glass, and allows only a transparent strip at the top of the windshield. We explain the limit for each window of your car before you book. Medical exemptions need a valid state certificate for that vehicle.

Ceramic or carbon window film?

Carbon film is a popular everyday choice with a deep matte look. Ceramic film typically rejects more heat and stays very clear, which many Florida drivers prefer for a car that sits in the sun. Ask us for the spec sheet of the film we recommend.

How long does window tint take?

It depends on the vehicle and the number of windows. Tint is usually a few hours. We confirm timing and aftercare in your quote.

What does the warranty cover?
