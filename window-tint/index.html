Which windows

Side windows

Front and rear. The ones up front can’t go as dark as the ones behind the driver.

Back window

Same film as the sides, cut for the rear glass. If your car already has dark factory glass back there, there may be little room to add film; we tell you before we start.

Windshield top strip

Florida only allows the transparent strip above the AS-1 line printed on the glass. The rest of the windshield stays bare.

Sunroof

Send a photo of it with the rest of the car and we’ll tell you if film fits it.

Why people do it here

  • Parked in the sun. Heat-rejecting film helps keep a car that sits in a Florida parking lot all afternoon from getting so hot, and it reduces glare. How much depends on the film; the maker’s spec sheet has the numbers.
  • Dash and seats. If the car sleeps outside, the dash and the seats are the first things the sun fades. UV-blocking film helps slow that down.
  • Some privacy. Behind the driver the law lets you go darker than up front, and that is where most of the privacy comes from.

Carbon or ceramic film?

If you only want to know which to pick: carbon for the everyday car, ceramic for the car that sits in the sun all day. The table says why.

Florida tint limits, window by window

Florida measures the finished window, glass and film together. A film’s own rating isn’t the number that counts, so before you pick we go over the minimum for each window of your car.

WindowPassenger carSUV, van or truck that counts as multipurpose*
Front side windows28%
Rear side windows and back window15%6%
WindshieldTransparent strip across the top, above the AS-1 line

*Minimum visible light that has to get through. “Multipurpose” is the law’s word for a vehicle built to carry 10 people or fewer that is made on a truck chassis or has special features for occasional off-road use. If we can’t tell how yours is classified, we use the passenger car limit.

Reflectivity is capped too, at 25% on the front side windows and 35% behind the driver, so most mirror films won’t pass. The installer has to put a label on the inside of the left door jamb that says the film complies, with the film’s name and the installer’s business name.

Tint that is too dark can mean a ticket for the driver (a non-moving violation in Florida).

Medical exemptions: the Florida Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles issues a certificate for one vehicle, and it can’t be moved to another. If you have one for this car, tell us before you order and show it to us. We don’t give medical advice or file exemptions.

Summary based on Florida Statutes 316.2951 to 316.2957 and 316.29545, checked October 5, 2026. It is not legal advice. Rules change, so confirm with the state before you order. Statute text: F.S. 316.2953 · 316.2954

Questions

Carbon or ceramic: which one should I get?

Carbon is the everyday choice and looks a little matte. Ceramic usually turns away more heat and is clearer to look through, which matters most on a car that sits in the sun. Send photos and tell us how you use the car, and we’ll point you to one.

How dark can I go?

It depends on the window, and the rear ones can go darker than the front ones. The limits are in the ‘Florida tint limits, window by window’ section of this page. If you drive an SUV or a truck, tell us which, because the rule changes with how the vehicle is classified.

Can you tint the whole windshield?

No. Florida only allows a transparent strip across the top, above the AS-1 line printed on the glass. That strip we can do.

What about the sunroof and the back window?

The back window, yes. For the sunroof, send us a photo and we’ll tell you if film fits it.

Is there a warranty?

Film makers write their own warranties, and the terms differ from brand to brand. Before you decide, read the terms for the exact film we quote you.

How much does it cost?

There isn’t one price. It depends on how many windows, the film, and whether old tint has to come off. Send photos and you’ll get a range; the final price is set once we see the car.
